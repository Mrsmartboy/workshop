# CODEGNAN LABS
## Website Product Vision — Developer Build Brief

A practical direction document for transforming the current workshop website into the digital front door for Codegnan Labs in colleges.

### NORTH STAR

> Build a platform that makes students say "I want Codegnan Labs in my college" and makes an HOD say "How do I organize this for my students?"

**Prepared for:** Codegnan Website / Product Development Team
**Website:** codegnan-workshop.vercel.app

---

## 1. What We Are Actually Building

Do not improve the current website as another course website.

Reimagine it as the digital front door for Codegnan Labs inside colleges.

The website should not feel like:

**Codegnan Training Institute -> Courses -> Events -> Register**

It should feel like:

**CODEGNAN LABS -> Campus Technology Experiences -> Students Explore, Build, Compete and Showcase -> Colleges Invite Us In**

Every design, content and development decision should answer two questions:

**Student reaction**
"This looks exciting. I want this to happen in my college."

**College reaction**
"This is relevant for our students. How do we organize it?"

**Codegnan outcome**
More campus enquiries, stronger social proof and a repeatable college acquisition engine.

---

## 2. Hero Experience: Make the Campus Proposition Obvious

The opening screen should communicate the product in seconds. Avoid a generic education headline and avoid overloading the hero with explanations.

### Suggested hero copy

> CODEGNAN LABS
>
> **Your Campus. Your Ideas. Let's Build Something.**
>
> Hands-on Workshops • Bootcamps • Hackathons
>
> [ Bring Codegnan to Your Campus ]   Explore Experiences

### Visual direction

- Use real Codegnan campus footage wherever possible: students coding, mentors helping, team discussions, demos, hackathon energy and winners.
- Prefer a short muted loop or high-energy photo collage over generic stock illustrations.
- Keep the primary CTA visible above the fold on both desktop and mobile.
- The brand hierarchy should read: Codegnan -> Codegnan Labs -> Workshops / Bootcamps / Hackathons.

Immediately below the hero, add a thin credibility/context strip:

> **BUILT FOR:** ENGINEERING COLLEGES | STUDENT COMMUNITIES | TECH CLUBS | CSE / IT DEPARTMENTS | PLACEMENT CELLS

## 3. Reframe Programs as Campus Experiences

Do not lead with a conventional "Our Programs" section. Call it **Choose Your Campus Experience**.

The visitor should understand the difference between each format without opening another page.

**WORKSHOP - EXPLORE**
2-6 hours. Give students a first hands-on experience with an emerging technology.
CTA: Explore Workshops.

**BOOTCAMP - BUILD**
1-5 days. Go deeper with guided learning, challenges and a working project.
CTA: Explore Bootcamps.

**HACKATHON - COMPETE**
1-2 days. Teams solve problems, build solutions and showcase what they created.
CTA: Explore Hackathons.

**Core experience philosophy:** EXPLORE -> BUILD -> COMPETE -> SHOWCASE

### Development thought

This journey can become a subtle scroll interaction: as visitors move through the page, the four verbs highlight in sequence. Keep motion restrained and fast; it should add energy without slowing the website.

---

## 4. Sell Outcomes, Not Technology Names

A static list such as Python, Java, AI, Cloud and Cybersecurity is technically correct but emotionally weak. Reframe technologies as things students can actually build.

**Suggested section title: What Could Your Students Build?**

- **Build Your First AI Assistant** — AI + Prompt Engineering
- **Build & Deploy a Website** — Web Development
- **Turn Data Into Insights** — Data Analytics
- **Build With Generative AI** — GenAI
- **Build an App Prototype** — App Development
- **Break. Secure. Learn.** — Cybersecurity

Each card should answer three things on click: who it is for, what students build, and what they leave with. That is more persuasive than showing a syllabus first.

---

## 5. Make the Website Relevant by Student Year

Colleges think in cohorts. Use that to make Codegnan Labs feel purpose-built rather than generic.

| Stage | Positioning | Recommended experiences |
|---|---|---|
| I Year | DISCOVER | AI Tools, Coding Foundations, GitHub, Build Your First Project |
| II Year | EXPLORE | Python, Java, Web, Data, AI |
| III Year | BUILD | Full Stack, APIs, Cloud, Advanced AI, Hackathons |
| IV Year | SHOWCASE | Portfolio, Industry Challenges, Hackathons, Placement-oriented Labs |

### Interactive thought

Allow users to select I / II / III / IV Year and dynamically filter suggested Labs. This is a useful enhancement for Phase 2 and will make the website feel consultative rather than brochure-like.

---

## 6. Build a "Design Your Campus Lab" Recommender

The strongest product enhancement is to turn the enquiry journey into a lightweight recommendation flow instead of sending every visitor to a generic contact form.

**STEP 1 — Who are the students?**
I Year / II Year / III Year / IV Year

**STEP 2 — How many?**
<50 / 50-100 / 100-300 / 300+

**STEP 3 — What format?**
Workshop / Bootcamp / Hackathon / Not Sure

**STEP 4 — Desired outcome**
Exposure / Skills / Projects / Competition / Placement Readiness

Then show a simple recommendation such as:

> **Recommended for your campus: AI Tools Workshop for I-Year Students**
>
> Suggested duration: 4 hours
> Expected outcome: students build a guided AI mini-project
>
> [ Talk to Codegnan Labs ]

This helps the college make a decision before sales contacts them. It also gives Codegnan better-qualified lead data.

---

## 7. Show Exactly How Codegnan Labs Comes to Campus

College decision-makers want predictability. Add a visual process that removes uncertainty around execution.

| Step | Description |
|---|---|
| 01 Tell us about your students | Year, branch, count and objective. |
| 02 We design the experience | Recommend technology, format and outcomes. |
| 03 Codegnan comes to campus | Mentors, content, challenges and execution. |
| 04 Students build | Hands-on participation, not passive lectures. |
| 05 Students showcase | Projects, certificates, recognition and outcomes. |

On desktop this can be horizontal; on mobile it should stack vertically. Keep it visual and brief.

## 8. Turn Real Campus Activity Into the Main Proof Layer

Replace generic testimonials and stock imagery with a strong **Codegnan Labs in Action** section.

Every real college program should become reusable website content.

**Campus Event Card**
Large image/video, college name, city, program type, duration and student count.

**Student Project Card**
Project name, what it does, team size, technology and optional GitHub/demo link.

**College Voice**
Real HOD/TPO quote with name, designation, institution and program context.

### Suggested content pattern

> CODEGNAN LABS x [COLLEGE NAME]
> AI Tools Workshop | 120 Students | 4 Hours
> See what students built ->

### Long-term flywheel

**College -> Lab -> Content -> Students Share -> Social Proof -> New College -> Lab**

---

## 9. Show What Students Build, Not Only What They Attend

Certificates matter, but projects create stronger proof. Position the end state as tangible output.

**They do not just attend. They build.**

- **AI Resume Analyzer** — Built during an AI Bootcamp. Show screenshot, team and stack.
- **Expense Tracker** — Built during a Python Workshop. Show output and learning evidence.
- **Campus Navigation App** — Built during a Hackathon. Show team, challenge and result.

### Recommended outcome chain

**PROJECT -> GITHUB / DEMO -> CERTIFICATE -> RECOGNITION**

Student emotion should also have a place on the page.

### Suggested direction

> Your next big idea might start here.
>
> Come curious. Try something new. Build with your friends. Ask questions. Break things. Fix them.
>
> Leave campus knowing: "I built this."

---

## 10. Make Hackathons Feel Like Events, Not Courses

Workshops, Bootcamps and Hackathons should not look like three versions of the same card. Hackathons need a more energetic visual language and dedicated landing page experience.

**IDEA -> TEAM -> BUILD -> MENTOR -> PITCH -> WIN**

**Countdown / Schedule**
Event start time, milestones and final pitch.

**Teams & Challenges**
Tracks, problem statements and participation details.

**Leaderboard / Winners**
Recognition, finalist showcases and winning projects.

The Hackathon page can use stronger motion, timers and competition visuals than the rest of the site. Keep the main Labs brand consistent, but let the event experience feel alive.

---

## 11. Recommended Information Architecture

Do not force all content onto the homepage. The homepage should inspire and route. Detail pages should answer the operational questions.

| Route | Purpose |
|---|---|
| /labs | Main Codegnan Labs home |
| /labs/workshops | Workshop catalogue and filters |
| /labs/bootcamps | Bootcamp catalogue and outcomes |
| /labs/hackathons | Hackathons and event experiences |
| /labs/colleges | Dedicated college decision-maker page |
| /labs/stories | Labs in Action, college stories and projects |
| /labs/ai-tools | Example individual experience page |

Each individual experience page should answer: Who is it for? What will students build? Duration? Prerequisites? What do they learn? What do they leave with? How does it run? How can a college bring it to campus?

## 12. Create a Dedicated "For Colleges" Conversion Page

This page should be what the partnerships team sends directly to Principals, Deans, HODs, TPOs and student-club leads.

> FOR COLLEGES
>
> **Turn your campus into a technology playground.**
>
> [ Plan a Codegnan Lab for Our Campus ]

The page should cover:

- Why Codegnan Labs
- Available formats and suggested durations
- Student capacity and year/branch fit
- Customisation options
- Execution model and what the college needs to provide
- Expected student outcomes
- Past campus stories / photos / testimonials
- FAQ
- Dedicated enquiry CTA

Use contextual CTA language across the website: Bring Codegnan to Your Campus, Plan a Campus Experience, Host a Hackathon, See What You Will Build, Help Me Choose. Avoid repeating generic "Register Now" and "Learn More" everywhere.

---

## 13. Design Mobile-First and Build for Sharing

Student discovery will often happen through WhatsApp, Instagram, LinkedIn, QR codes and college groups. Mobile is not a secondary layout.

**Mobile UX**
Fast load, compact copy, swipeable experience cards, compressed media and visible CTA.

**Sticky Action**
Persistent bottom CTA such as "Bring Codegnan to Campus" or "Explore Labs."

**Shareability**
Allow students to share an experience page with their HOD, club or classmates via WhatsApp.

Every completed campus event should ideally get a shareable page containing event details, photos, projects, winners and testimonials. That turns each event into marketing for the next event.

## 14. Development Phases and Priority

| Phase | Focus | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| PHASE 1 | Positioning, hero, experience cards, college CTA, real content, remove placeholders, mobile cleanup | Launch-ready Codegnan Labs identity |
| PHASE 2 | Experience pages, For Colleges page, Labs in Action, student projects, year-based filters | Stronger conversion and proof |
| PHASE 3 | Campus recommender, dynamic event pages, share flows, analytics, scalable content model | College acquisition platform |

Phase 1 should not get blocked waiting for advanced functionality. The immediate goal is to make the proposition clear, credible and conversion-ready.
